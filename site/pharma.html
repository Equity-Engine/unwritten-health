The same UK panel works at both ends. Members who live with the condition review your protocol, invitation and visit plan before they're final. Once you're recruiting, we refer those who pass your screening questions and say yes, and follow every referral through.

Patient involvement

Your ethics application asks what patients changed. Most answers are one paragraph.

The IRAS form asks how many people you involved, what they did, and how it made your clinical trial more acceptable to the people taking part. The Health Research Authority's checklist for applicants says: "Participant information developed without public involvement will not normally receive a favourable opinion from REC."

We put your protocol synopsis, information sheet, consent form and invitation in front of members before the plan is fixed, and hand you a report that answers the question properly.

How long involvement takes

When we already hold your condition, a pilot on one document usually takes about ten working days from the day you send the document to the day the report lands; if we have to recruit first, allow about four weeks. We quote the exact number of working days before anything starts, and we hold to it. Survey answers come back in hours to days; the report takes longer because we write it.

If you're a UK affiliate

Global protocols arrive fixed. What you own is the UK version of the information sheet, the invitation, the site list and the recruitment plan, and that's where we work: put the UK documents in front of UK members in the weeks you have before submission, and hand global a report that shows the UK affiliate did it properly.

If you're in patient engagement

You're asked to bring patient insight into clinical trial design, regulatory strategy and product development, at every stage of a medicine's life. Advisory boards and roundtables give you a handful of voices a few times a year. We give you people living with the condition, chosen to reflect who has it, whenever you have a question, and a report showing who said what and what changed. It grows with your portfolio, and it sits alongside your advocacy partnerships rather than replacing them.

If you're in market access

When a submission needs patient-reported burden, treatment acceptability or the experience of a particular group, we ask members through surveys, video diaries and voice notes and break the answers down by age, sex, ethnicity and deprivation. It's patient-reported data collected for your question, written up as an appendix or as briefing for patient experts. It isn't medical records data, and every report says so. A survey of 300 members with video diaries is quoted per piece of work.

If you're in clinical operations

We refer members who pass your screening questions and say yes, with a best time to call, and follow every referral through. Anyone the site hasn't reached by day 7 goes to the site that week, then to your report a week later if still not reached. We don't promise enrolment, and eligibility stays the site's call. Before sites open, members can also review your recruitment materials, and we can show you where people with the condition live. If your CRO drafts the documents, the CRO can send them to us and receive the report.

Compliance

Members are paid £27.50 an hour, in line with NIHR's rate for public involvement, for answering our surveys. Never for their answers, never for joining a clinical trial, and the check-ins after a referral are unpaid. Two consents before any detail leaves us: a standing opt-in to hear about clinical trials, then a yes to one named clinical trial. Your material stays inside our platform.

Safety

Members are told when they join how to report a side effect to their doctor and to the MHRA's Yellow Card scheme. Every survey answer, voice note and video diary is screened by our team before it reaches you. Anything that mentions a side effect, a product complaint or a safety concern about a named medicine is passed to your pharmacovigilance contact within one working day, and we close every piece of work with a reconciliation log of what was passed on and when.

Alongside Be Part of Research

If you're recruiting through NIHR's free registry, we'll put your invitation email and study pages in front of members first and tell you where people stop reading, what puts them off, and what's missing. On Be Part of Research, research teams report how many volunteers were interested and how many took part. Our After part adds the patient's own account of where things stand, with the site's note beside it.
