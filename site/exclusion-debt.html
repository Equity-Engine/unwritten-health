Exclusion debt

Exclusion debt: what it costs to leave people out of clinical research.

Definition. Exclusion debt is the compounding cost of designing and running clinical research without the people it's meant for. Missed recruitment targets, extra months, referrals nobody answers, and research that never reaches the places where illness is most common are all repayments of it. Like technical debt, it's cheap to avoid early and expensive to pay later.

How it shows up in UK research

4 in 10

Of NIHR-funded clinical trials reported from 2017 to 2020, nearly 4 in 10 didn't reach their original recruitment target, and 37% needed more time.

Jacques et al., BMJ Open, 2022.

90,000

From 2013 to 2018, the areas of England with the most long-term illness had the fewest people taking part in research. To match, about 90,000 people would have had to be recruited in different areas.

NIHR Evidence, on Bower et al., September 2020.

1 in 4

When the Health Research Authority checked 398 applications given a favourable ethics opinion in 2023, about a quarter hadn't involved the public at all.

HRA, Public involvement in health and social care research in the UK: report 2023.

Referrals that go unanswered add to it too. No published figure covers UK commercial clinical trials, but in a 2017 Tufts survey of 2,000 doctors and nurses, mainly in the US and Europe, nearly 30% of doctors and 45% of nurses said they never heard back after referring a patient to a clinical trial (Tufts CSDD, in Applied Clinical Trials, January 2017).

What the UK rules say, and don't

The HRA expects public involvement in all research. Participant information developed without it "will not normally receive a favourable opinion" from an NHS ethics committee.

The new UK clinical trials regulations came into force on 28 April 2026. The HRA's diversity and inclusion guidance "is not a legal requirement", and its pilot of inclusion and diversity plans closed in December 2025.

Sources: HRA, public involvement guidance; HRA, clinical trials regulations.
