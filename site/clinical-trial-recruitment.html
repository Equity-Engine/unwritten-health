We follow every referral through to randomisation, and tell you which ones went nowhere and why. Patient recruitment in the UK: what you can ask for.

  1. Patient feasibility. Free, the same working day: how many members near your sites match on what they've told us, by condition, stage and postcode district, how many have already said yes, and how they break down by ethnicity, age, sex and area deprivation. Pre-screened, never eligible. Free patient feasibility
  2. Site choice by postcode district. Where people with the condition live, from the first part of members' postcodes, and what would make taking part possible: travel, time off work, childcare.
  3. Invitation testing. Members who live with the condition review your invitation, visit plan and Be Part of Research pages before they go out, through patient engagement, before the ethics submission.
  4. Referrals. Members who pass your screening questions and say yes to your clinical trial, referred through the site's own referral link, with the yes recorded and a best time to call.
  5. Evidence for your Inclusion and Diversity Plan. Who is affected near your sites, and how you'll recruit and retain them, from real people. Questions 1, 3 and 4
  6. Every patient followed to randomisation. One line per patient each week, with randomisation checked against your own record.
1

Referrals: pre-screened patients, sent to the site

We put your screening questions to members who live with the condition. Those who pass and say yes to your clinical trial are referred through the site's own referral link, with a best time to call.

Eligibility stays the site's call. Conditions are self-reported, with qualifying questions, so a referral means the member is pre-screened: they passed your screening questions. Whether they can take part is for the site to decide.

Two consents before any detail leaves us: a standing opt-in to hear about clinical trials, then a yes to one named clinical trial. The site's informed consent is a third step, and never ours.

Sponsors never learn who members are: only we, and the site a member says yes to, know their name.

2

We stay with every patient we refer

We check in with every member we refer: at day seven, after the visit and a month on. Anyone the site hasn't reached by day 7 goes back to the site that week, so the site hears first and can add its own note. If they're still not reached a week later, they go into the sponsor's report with the site's note beside them. It's a record of where each patient is, never a score for the site.

We follow each one from the first invitation to randomisation, across seven steps: Referred, Contacted, Booked, Attended, Consented, Screened, Randomised. Screen failures come back to us with the question that failed, so the screener gets tighter. You get one line per patient each week: where they are across the seven steps, where the patient says things stand, the site's note beside it, and why anyone stopped. Randomisation is checked against your own record.

Why patient recruitment should follow every referral through.

30% and 45%

In Tufts CSDD surveys from 2015 to 2016, nearly 30% of doctors and 45% of nurses said they never had initial or follow-up contact from site staff after referring a patient to a clinical trial.

Tufts CSDD, reported in Applied Clinical Trials, January 2017.

35%

Of 133 cancer patients a clinical trial finder service helped, 35% got a reply through the contact details listed on ClinicalTrials.gov, and 47% once other channels were tried.

DIA Global Forum, September 2024.

12%

From 2013 to 2018, recruitment to NIHR research in England was disproportionately low in the areas with the most long-term and mental health conditions. Across the 15 research networks, about 12% of recruitment (394,290 of 3.27 million recruits) would have had to move to match where illness is.

Bower et al., 2020, summarised by NIHR Evidence.

39%

Of 174 randomised clinical trials reported in the NIHR Journals Library from 2017 to 2020, 39% (67) missed their original recruitment target, and 37% needed more time.

Jacques et al., BMJ Open, 2022.

We don't claim that following patients up raises enrolment: we haven't found controlled evidence that it does. It gives the team the facts, not a promised result.

How we do it.

  1. The record is the patient's own account at each check-in, with the site's note beside it, sent to the sponsor referral by referral.
  2. The site hears first about anyone not yet reached, a week before the sponsor does, and its own note goes beside them.
  3. The same members review the protocol and the invitation before the protocol is final.
  4. Members built from the communities clinical trials miss, profiled to postcode district, with each condition broken down by age, sex, ethnicity and deprivation.
  5. We engage members continuously and in the moment, so what we know about them is current.

Alongside Be Part of Research

On Be Part of Research, research teams report how many volunteers were interested and how many took part. Our follow-through adds the patient's own account of where things stand, with the site's note beside it.

What members are paid

Members are paid for their time when a client funds the work, at £27.50 an hour, in line with NIHR's rate for public involvement. Nobody is paid for joining a clinical trial, and the check-ins after a referral are unpaid.
