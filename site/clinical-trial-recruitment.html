The Before part is patient involvement, and you can buy it on its own.

1

Through: pre-screened referrals

We put your screening questions to members who live with the condition. Those who pass and say yes are referred to your site, with a best time to call.

Eligibility stays the site's call. Conditions are self-declared, with qualifying questions, so a referral means the member passed your screening questions. Whether they can take part is for the site to decide.

Two consents before any detail leaves us: a standing opt-in to hear about clinical trials, then a yes to one named clinical trial.

2

After: we stay with every referral

We check in with every member we refer. Anyone the site hasn't reached by day 7 goes to the site that week, so the site sees its own stalled referrals first. If they're still not reached, they go into the sponsor's report a week later.

You get a line a week per referral: where the patient says things stand, the site's note beside it, and why anyone stopped.

Why follow every referral through.

30% and 45%

In a 2017 Tufts survey of 2,000 doctors and nurses, mainly in the US and Europe, nearly 30% of doctors and 45% of nurses said they never heard back after referring a patient to a clinical trial.

Tufts CSDD, in Applied Clinical Trials, January 2017.

35%

Of 133 cancer patients a clinical trial finder service helped, 35% got a reply through the contact details listed on ClinicalTrials.gov, and 47% once other channels were tried.

DIA Global Forum, September 2024.

4 in 10

Of NIHR-funded clinical trials reported from 2017 to 2020, nearly 4 in 10 didn't reach their original recruitment target, and 37% needed more time.

Jacques et al., BMJ Open, 2022.

We don't claim that following patients up raises enrolment: we haven't found controlled evidence that it does. It gives the team the facts, not a promised result.

How we do it.

  1. The record is the patient's own account at each check-in, with the site's note beside it, sent to the sponsor referral by referral.
  2. The site sees its own stalled referrals a week before the sponsor does.
  3. The same members review the protocol and the invitation before the protocol is final.
  4. A UK panel, profiled to postcode district, with each condition broken down by age, sex, ethnicity and deprivation.
  5. Members are paid for their time and asked again over time, so what we know grows.

Alongside Be Part of Research

On Be Part of Research, research teams report how many volunteers were interested and how many took part. The After part adds the patient's own account of where things stand, with the site's note beside it.

What members are paid

Members are paid £27.50 an hour, in line with NIHR's rate for public involvement, for answering our surveys. Never for joining a clinical trial, and the check-ins after a referral are unpaid.
