Patient involvement for clinical trials, medicines, devices, digital health and NHS services

If it's built for patients, patients should shape it.

We put your clinical trial plan, product or materials in front of people living with the condition, recruited to match who actually has it by age, sex, ethnicity and background, and we publish how close we are. You get back a report showing what they said, what you changed, and what you didn't.

The problem

Most research still can't show what patients changed.

Patient and public involvement (PPI) groups do good work with very little. The problem is scale: a group of ten can't tell you how a thousand people with the condition would read your information sheet, and it can't be broken down by age, ethnicity or deprivation to show who said what.

And a lot of research still goes ahead without any involvement at all. When the Health Research Authority checked 398 applications given a favourable ethics opinion in 2023, about a quarter hadn't involved the public at all. Clinical trials were less likely to have involved people than other research, and commercial research less likely than non-commercial. Many of the applications that did involve people didn't explain how it made the research more relevant to the people taking part.

The people most often left out are the people the product or clinical trial is for.

What we do differently

Matched to the condition, not to whoever signed up.

Most panels are whoever happened to join. Ours is recruited so that, for each condition, the people you hear from reflect the people who actually have it, and we publish how close we are, condition by condition.

Type 2 diabetes is the clearest example. It's two to four times more likely in people of South Asian, African-Caribbean and Black African descent. A panel that looks like the general public is wrong before the first question is asked. Of our 1,900 members with type 2 diabetes, 16% are Asian and 5% are Black, against 9% and 4% of the population of England and Wales. Where we're short of the condition, the panel page says so.

Where we fit

Before the design is fixed, not after.

Registries like NIHR's Be Part of Research help you find participants once your clinical trial is approved. They're made up of whoever chooses to sign up.

We come earlier, and we're built differently. Our panel is matched to the people who have the condition, and it tells you what they think, not just whether they're interested. We help you shape the plan, the information sheet and the invitation with the people they're for, so the strategy is stronger before anyone is asked to take part.

Why the right people matter

Involving people who have the condition makes a measurable difference.

A review of 26 studies found that involving patients was linked to better odds of enrolling participants in clinical trials: an odds ratio of 1.16 overall. In the small group of studies where the people involved had the condition being studied, the figure was 3.14, though with so few studies the range around it is wide.

We don't claim involvement will do that for your clinical trial. We make sure the people involved are the right ones, and we show you exactly who they were.
