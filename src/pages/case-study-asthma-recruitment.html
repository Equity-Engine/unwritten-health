Case study · Our pilot · Asthma

A phase 2 asthma clinical trial, from the count to the last referral.

Six UK sites, 40 participants to randomise and five months to do it. This is how our pilot ran, week by week. The sponsor can't be named.

The clinical trial

A US biotech's phase 2 clinical trial in moderate-to-severe asthma, with six UK sites: two in London, and one each in Manchester, Birmingham, Leicester and Glasgow. The UK target was 40 randomised participants within five months. The study was delayed, and recruitment was proving difficult without a central patient recruitment strategy.

Why it's hard. Sites are fishing in a small pool: their own clinic lists. And the people asthma hits hardest are often the ones clinic lists reach least.

39%

Of NIHR-funded clinical trials published from 2017 to 2020, 39% didn't reach their original recruitment target.

Jacques et al., BMJ Open, 2022.

59% and 34%

In a national cohort in Scotland, Pakistani men were admitted to hospital or died from asthma 59% more often than White Scottish men, and Indian men 34% more often, adjusting for age.

Sheikh et al., BMC Medicine, 2016.

4 times

In England, asthma death rates are four times higher in children from deprived backgrounds than in their better-off peers.

RCPCH, State of Child Health 2026.

Week 0

The count, the same working day

The sponsor sent us the six site postcodes and the eligibility criteria, along with the protocol. The same working day, they got our reach:

900
members living with asthma in the UK at the time
720
of them within 15 miles of the six sites
180
of those on a medium or high-dose inhaled steroid, with two or more courses of oral steroids in the last year, by their own account
135
of those had already said yes to hearing about clinical trials

How they broke down: 24% Asian, 9% Black, 31% from the most deprived fifth of areas, median age 47, set against the mix in the sites' catchments.

That count was free, and it was real people, not a forecast. It told the sponsor, before they spent anything, whether six sites and five months was a plan or a hope.

Weeks 1 to 3

Before the ethics submission

200 members with asthma reviewed the invitation, the visit schedule and the plain-words explanation of placebo on screen, and 20 recorded voice diaries on what would stop them joining.

What came back was mostly practical: the chance of a dummy medicine when their current inhaler was working, visits on weekdays only, and travel to a site across town when they already had a clinic they trusted.

So the sponsor updated the protocol and the materials before the ethics submission:

  • Visit windows that include early mornings and Saturdays.
  • Travel paid up front, not claimed back.
  • A one-page explanation of placebo in plain English, Urdu, Bengali and Polish.
2
Week 8 on

Referrals, followed through

Once ethics approval came through, we put the screening questions agreed with the sponsor to members. Those who passed and said yes to this named clinical trial were referred to the site, with a best time to call.

Before any referral was passed on, both consents were in place: a standing yes to hearing about clinical trials, then a yes to this one. The site confirmed the diagnosis: we said "pre-screened", never "eligible".

We checked in with every member we referred at day seven and again after the screening visit. Where nobody had called, the site heard first. Every Monday the sponsor received one line per person, across six steps (referred, contacted, booked, attended, screened, randomised), with the member's own account beside the site's note.

The result

At the end of the pilot, the sponsor was able to confirm to its counterparts that UK recruitment finished on time. Every referral was accounted for, including the ones that went nowhere and why. And the UK participants looked like the people who have the condition, with the numbers to show an ethics committee.

The same numbers answer questions 1, 3 and 4 of the HRA's Inclusion and Diversity Plan: who is affected by the condition, what the recruitment goals are, and the plan to recruit and retain them.

How it was paid for

The sponsor paid a fee per randomised participant, checked against the site's own record and capped at the target, with a referral commitment and money back if we fell short of it.

There was no advertising spend: every referral came from members who had already joined.
