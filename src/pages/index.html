Who we're built for

The people research misses.

Research gets done on the people who are easiest to find, and the medicine then gets used by everyone else. The people left out are usually the ones carrying the most illness: people from ethnic minority backgrounds, people in the most deprived areas, people in rural areas, older people, people with more than one condition, people who don't read English.

So we build the membership from those communities, and we measure it: most members are from those communities today, and you see the breakdown for your condition in patient feasibility. Why it's a clinical operations question, not a charity ask

A GCP principle, in UK law since 28 April 2026

ICH E6(R3), principle 1.4: participant selection "should be representative of the population groups that the investigational product is intended to benefit". The Clinical Trials Regulations (SI 2025/538) require clinical trials of medicines to follow the ICH GCP principles.

The HRA and MHRA Inclusion and Diversity Plan

Not mandatory, but encouraged, and from early 2027 more sponsors will be asked to volunteer one. Patient feasibility gives you real people near your sites for questions 1, 3 and 4: who is affected, your recruitment goals, and how you'll recruit and retain them.

NHS boards, NIHR funders and the regulators abroad

NHS boards must be assured their research recruitment is "representative of the communities who will benefit". Inclusive research design is a condition of NIHR funding. The EU Clinical Trials Regulation asks whether participants represent the people likely to use the medicine, and the FDA recommends enrolling a "representative population".

ICH E6(R3); SI 2025/538; HRA, Inclusion and Diversity Plan guidance, updated 2 November 2026; MHRA, pilot findings, 3 November 2026; NHS England, Framework for NHS boards, 26 August 2026; NIHR funding guidance; EU Regulation 536/2014, recital 14; FDA, Enhancing Participation in Clinical Trials, December 2025.

Trust

Lines we won't cross.

  • No brand work. Members never promote a product, act as ambassadors or mentors, or appear in a company's marketing.
  • No selling identities. Sponsors never learn who members are: only we, and the site a member says yes to, know their name. We never sell member data.
  • No pay for joining. Members are paid for their time when a client funds the work, at a fair rate for defined work, never contingent on what they say. Membership itself is unpaid, and so are the check-ins after a referral. Nobody is paid for joining us or for joining a clinical trial.
  • No synthetic patients. Every answer, including the AI's, traces back to real members.
  • Two consents before any detail leaves us: a standing opt-in to hear about clinical trials, then a yes to one named clinical trial. The site's informed consent is a third step, and never ours.
  • No tokenism. No member stands for a community, and we're never sold as an add-on to someone else's membership.
