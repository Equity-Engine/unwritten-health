About

Built by someone who's lived what happens when people go unheard.

Unwritten Health is a clinical trial recruitment and patient engagement company, building longitudinal data sets from under-represented communities. It was founded by Ashish Rishi. He spent nearly ten years running a clinical trial recruitment business, which he sold in 2024, and he's from one of the communities clinical trials miss. He sits on the scientific advisory board of IHI READI, a Horizon Europe project on representative clinical research.

My story

Most people know me as the founder of a patient recruitment company here in the UK. I built it to reach the communities clinical trials usually miss, long before it became fashionable. I've spent my career inside healthcare agencies, across research and development, brand launches, market access, medical affairs and commercial strategy. I've seen what works, what doesn't, and who gets left behind.

Why I keep going runs deeper than work. I'm the son of immigrants. My dad came to the UK in the early 70s and my mum in the late 70s. They had an arranged marriage; the first time she saw him was their wedding day. They didn't have much, but they had grit. My mum worked in factories, making ice cream and testing vinyl records, before moving into a clerical job. My dad became a TV engineer. He always said he'd had the choice between TVs and computers, and he thought TVs were the future. They worked twelve-hour shifts, overtime and weekends, and skipped holidays, so I could have a better shot than they had.

That hard work came at a cost. My dad was diagnosed with prostate cancer at 52. My mum was diagnosed with thyroid cancer at 48. Both too young, and both let down by the system. My dad knew something was wrong and pushed for answers for over a year. By the time anyone listened, it was terminal.

I was 17, sitting in a hospital room, when the consultant told me and my mum he had six months to live. He handed us a leaflet and said we'd get a follow-up. No pathway, no options. We had family and friends, but no advocates. We didn't know what questions to ask. We didn't know what we didn't know.

My dad held on for six more years. He knew he wouldn't see me marry, so he wanted to see me graduate. He died six months before I did, and that will always sit heavy with me. My mum didn't spot her own cancer and didn't know how to ask for help. She just kept going, like so many working-class people do, until her body made her stop.

That's why I do this. Not because I read a regulation, and not because someone hired me to, but because I've lived what happens when people are unseen, unheard and uncared for. Every keynote, every campaign, every late night has been about representation. I've built things, burnt out and rebuilt. I'm still here, and I'm still building.

Ash. Son of immigrants. Built from graft, fuelled by grief, powered by purpose.

How we started

We started as a patient experience company, built to hear from people about living with their condition, their care and clinical trials. That's why we know each member's stage, postcode district and background, and whether they want to hear about clinical trials. Recruitment grew out of that, because buyers asked for the people as well as what they told us. Today we're a clinical trial recruitment and patient engagement company, built from the communities clinical trials miss: 15,000 people in the UK (November 2026, and growing daily), and expanding into Germany, France, Spain, the Netherlands, Portugal, Ireland and Poland.

How people join us

People join us through the patient groups and community organisations they already trust, and we're there in person as well as online. Keeping that trust is our job.

Every one of the 15,000 joined through community outreach; we've never run paid search. Patient groups are partners in the design, not places to find patients: we bring them in before the protocol is final, and they hear what happened to every member referred. A partner tells someone about us and hands over nothing: no name, no diagnosis, no list.

What a partner group gets

Their members' experience back every quarter, in their words. A seat on our advisory group, with a veto over how their members' data is used. And the same report on their members' referrals that the sponsor gets.

What we never ask of a partner

Exclusivity, promoting a product, their membership list, or an opinion they don't hold. Every partnership has a written agreement, built to the ABPI Code.

Who does what

Equity Engine{{EXT}} is our member platform, where people join, built for under-served communities from day one. Unwritten Health is the company clients work with. Counted, our community interest company, explains research to communities rarely included in it; it sells nothing and recruits for nothing. Footnote is the framework our AI runs under, and In Their Words is our LinkedIn newsletter.

Our principle

Sustainable, not extractive.

It's the idea the business hangs on: we don't take from patients and walk away. Research can be extractive, taking stories and data and leaving nothing behind. Ours is built to be sustainable: relationships that last years, and the data gets better because of it.

In practice, that means data back to partner groups every quarter, and members told what changed because of them. They tell us more, they answer again, and the ones referred into clinical trials already know us.

What we believe

Anything developed for a patient should be shaped by patients before it goes out, starting with the people research usually misses. Research gets done on the people who are easiest to find, and the medicine then gets used by everyone else. Not a handful of people, however good. Not whoever signed up. Enough people, from the communities clinical trials miss, to show who said what.

The name

Most of what's made for patients is written without them. That's the unwritten part.
